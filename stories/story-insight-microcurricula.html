Microcurricula as Mutual Aid

How modular lessons that fit inside a backpack keep displaced students tethered to STEM pathways.

Our curriculum team has been prototyping "microcurricula": clusters of lessons that can be completed in 30 minutes, require minimal materials, and are easy to pass between learners. Here is what we have learned.

Category
Pedagogical Insights
Published
February 15, 2024
Authored by
Aisha Dualeh, Director of Curriculum

This facilitator insight sits inside our English for Academic Purposes toolkit, offering concrete moves that keep language objectives visible while delivering hands-on content.

Design commitments

Microcurricula emerged from a request by students living between community shelters in Amman. They needed lessons that could survive transit, language shifts, and inconsistent internet. We adopted three design commitments:

  1. Portability. Every lesson fits on two pages and can be folded into a pocket-sized booklet.
  2. Reciprocity. Instructions include prompts for learners to teach the concept to someone else.
  3. Radical choice. Students pick the sequence that matches their emotional bandwidth that day.

Example: “Fraction Kitchens”

In one module, learners design a community kitchen menu using fractional measurements. The kit includes fraction cards, blank recipe templates, and a cultural memory prompt (“Which dish reminds you of home?”). Students calculate ingredient adjustments while sharing stories about food migration.

What educators noticed

Facilitators across our Virtual Studio shared quick reflections after piloting the microcurricula:

  • Students with irregular attendance were able to re-enter without feeling behind.
  • Peer-to-peer teaching flourished because lessons included “teach-forward” scripts.
  • Trauma-informed grounding practices baked into the introductions reduced anxiety.
“These kits behave like mutual aid packages,” says Aisha. “They move through the community, changing hands and absorbing new stories along the way.”

What’s next

We are releasing four new microcurricula this spring focused on algebraic thinking, civic design, and multilingual storytelling. Each will include facilitator audio guides and suggestions for adapting the lesson to offline or low-power settings. Educators can request the kits through our partner portal starting in May.
