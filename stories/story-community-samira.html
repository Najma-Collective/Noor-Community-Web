Samira's Listening Labs Changed How We Welcome Newcomers

A volunteer mentor reimagines intake meetings so that young arrivals start their Noor journey feeling heard, not assessed.

Samira Ahmed, a volunteer mentor based in Toronto, noticed that our intake meetings felt more like interviews than invitations. Her solution, Listening Labs, has now become a staple across Noor hubs.

Category
Student Voices
Published
April 9, 2024
Location
Toronto, Canada
Featuring
Samira Ahmed (Volunteer Mentor)

This update showcases how Noor's English programmes scale: linking community infrastructure, donor support, and educator leadership to rich language learning experiences.

From intake forms to listening labs

Samira joined Noor during the winter lockdowns, facilitating virtual study halls for recently arrived Syrian and Somali students. By the spring term she was co-leading orientation sessions. “I could see our values on paper,” she recalls, “but the first encounter still felt like a checklist.”

She began prototyping short circles she called Listening Labs. Each lab pairs two learners, a mentor, and a few prompts that centre storytelling instead of paperwork. The prompts ask about the songs that travelled with them, their previous schools, and the emotions connected to studying in a new language.

"When we start with memories and metaphors, young people realise they are bringing assets (not deficits) into the classroom."

What changed for students

Attendance data showed that students who experienced Listening Labs were 22% more likely to return for the second intake session. The labs also shortened the time it took staff to co-create learning plans because students were more forthcoming with their academic transcripts and previous interests.

  • Students completed self-assessments three days earlier on average.
  • Families requested more bilingual resources after hearing peers do the same.
  • Mentors reported higher confidence in facilitating restorative conversations.

Scaling the practice

Listening Labs have now been adopted in Noor’s Nairobi and Amman hubs. Samira co-designed a facilitator guide with our pedagogy team, including sensory warm-ups, trauma-aware check-ins, and a note-taking template. The guide is shared with new volunteers during onboarding and is available in English, Arabic, and Somali.

Next, Samira wants to bring alumni into the process. “Imagine a circle where a current student sits with a Noor graduate who is now in university,” she says. “That’s when belonging becomes generational.”
