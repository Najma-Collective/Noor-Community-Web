Community Commons Launches After-School Pods

Caregivers in Tunis redesigned their municipal library to host solar-powered pods for arts, numeracy, and storytelling.

What began as a weekly caregiver meeting is now a full-fledged community campus. The Commons collective in Tunis transformed a vacant mezzanine into four micro-studios that stay open until 10 p.m.

Category
Community News
Published
May 10, 2024
Location
Tunis, Tunisia
Featuring
Community Commons Cooperative

This update showcases how Noor's English programmes scale, linking community infrastructure, donor support, and educator leadership to rich language learning experiences.

Solar pods built by neighbors

Local electricians, alumni, and aunties salvaged shelving to build lightweight frames, then layered recycled canvas and solar films donated by a partner university. Each pod seats eight students and is wired for low-voltage lighting, laptops, and speakers. The pods snap together in less than five minutes, letting librarians reclaim the mezzanine during daytime hours.

Programming designed by the community

The Commons collective split the nightly schedule into three strands: numeracy labs, arts studios, and storytelling pods. Elders facilitate reading circles, volunteers host financial literacy clinics, and Noor facilitators drop in virtually to coach groups on project planning. Caregivers coordinate a rotating snack fund using mobile money.

  • 72 students signed up within the first week; 60% are girls.
  • Two pods now stream our math studio curriculum directly from Dakar.
  • Weekly open-mic nights invite families to celebrate progress.

Next steps

The collective is negotiating with the municipality to keep the pods open during exam season. Noor engineers are documenting the build process so partner cities can replicate the design with locally available materials.

“The pods make it obvious that libraries are for living, not just for silence,” says Amel, a parent organizer. “We’re building the kind of infrastructure that students deserve.”
