Elias Keeps His Radio Diary Rolling Through Exams

A 16-year-old archivist in Amman uses late-night radio segments to stay grounded in Arabic, English, and community storytelling.

Elias first joined Noor to improve his English essays; he stayed because the radio studio inside our partner hub gave him a mic, a soundboard, and a reason to interview his elders every week.

Category
Student Voices
Published
May 8, 2024
Location
Amman, Jordan
Featuring
Elias Mansour (Student Archivist)

Every activity in this Student Voice feature is anchored to Noor's English language pathway, so learners log new vocabulary, practice academic sentence frames, and rehearse oral fluency while they build.

Radio diaries as a literacy plan

The homework brief was simple: narrate one page each night. Elias reimagined it into a full radio diary, scripting three-minute episodes that he records on an old handheld mic. Each episode is produced in Arabic and English so he can practise code switching whilst staying rooted in family phrases. He layers background sounds from his neighbourhood (coffee cups, tram bells, birdsong) so that vocabulary comes tethered to memory.

His facilitator, Zahra, uses the transcripts to track grammar progress without flattening his voice. Together they color-code verbs, annotate metaphors, and catalogue new idioms that community members teach Elias during interviews.

“When I press record I stop thinking about perfection. I just follow the story.”

Building an intergenerational newsroom

Every Thursday the Noor hub transforms into a mini newsroom. Elias invites aunties, neighbors, and other students to co-host the diary. They storyboard with sticky notes, practice mic technique, and learn to engineer with open-source software. Parents who originally dropped off snacks now stick around to record healing recipes and lullabies.

  • 32 unique guests have contributed to the diary since January.
  • Four other students now rotate as sound engineers and caption writers.
  • The episodes double as listening exercises for Noor's literacy pods.

What comes next

Elias wants to syndicate the diary to the rooftop loudspeakers that announce community events. Our team is helping him build a Creative Commons license, archive releases on the Noor website, and translate each episode into text-message summaries for families with limited data.

"The diary proves exams are just one version of accountability," Zahra says. "What Elias is building is memory, something standardised tests can't measure."
