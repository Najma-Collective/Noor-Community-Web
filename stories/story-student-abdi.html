Abdi's Repair Bus Teaches Torque and Tenderness

A refurbished school bus now houses Nairobi's most joyful apprenticeship program, pairing physics with mutual aid.

When Abdi realized his neighbors were tossing broken appliances out of frustration, he convinced his facilitator to let him turn an unused bus into a roving repair cafe.

Category
Student Voices
Published
April 28, 2024
Location
Nairobi, Kenya
Featuring
Abdi Muhammed (Repair Lead)

Every activity in this Student Voice feature is anchored to Noor's English language pathway, so learners log new vocabulary, practice academic sentence frames, and rehearse oral fluency while they build.

Designing a syllabus on wheels

The bus stops at three estates each week. Students sign up for two-hour shifts where they diagnose radios, bikes, and sewing machines. Every repair begins with a "care interview" that documents how the object is used and what memories it holds. Those notes become literacy exercises for younger volunteers.

Apprenticeship meets accountability

Abdi mapped each station (intake, diagnostics, soldering, testing, and storytelling) so that teens rotate between technical and relational tasks. Each rotation includes a physics mini-lesson. The curriculum now covers torque, current, and pricing transparency, giving students language to negotiate future gigs.

Neighborhood impact

In four months the crew repaired 312 items and trained 26 younger siblings. Local vendors donate spare parts, and elders bring recipes to keep energy levels up. "The repairs are an excuse to talk about dignity," Abdi says. "You can't torque a bolt without also listening to its owner."
