Restorative Feedback Loops in Hybrid Classrooms

Feedback can behave like a gift economy, if we scaffold the rituals and language that make generosity safe.

For multilingual learners toggling between online and in-person sessions, feedback is often reduced to emojis or redlines. Our facilitators experimented with a restorative protocol that restored humanity to the process.

Category
Pedagogical Insights
Published
January 30, 2024
Authored by
Khaled Mansour, Head of Facilitation

This facilitator insight sits inside our English for Academic Purposes toolkit, offering concrete moves that keep language objectives visible while delivering hands-on content.

Why we needed a new approach

Students told us their assignments returned with either overwhelming comments or none at all. The feedback cadence also shifted drastically when classes moved between WhatsApp, Zoom, and physical study halls. We decided to co-create a ritual that could travel between formats.

The three-part ritual

The new protocol unfolds in three short rounds:

  1. Arrival. Everyone names one feeling and one asset they bring to the circle. This grounds the group and invites self-recognition.
  2. Inquiry. The learner asks for the kind of feedback they need (“help me make this clearer,” “tell me where you felt confused”). Peers respond with questions before offering advice.
  3. Gifting. Each participant offers one "gift" they are leaving with: an idea, a resource, or an affirmation. These are captured in a shared doc or audio note.

Results across hubs

Within a month we saw tangible shifts:

  • Assignment resubmissions increased by 31% because students knew exactly what to revise.
  • Facilitators spent less time “correcting” and more time connecting students to peer mentors.
  • WhatsApp voice notes replaced long text threads, making it easier for emerging readers to participate.
“The gifting round reminded us that feedback is a circle, not a straight line from teacher to student,” Khaled shared.
