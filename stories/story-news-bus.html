The Study Bus Brings Modular Labs to Desert Towns

Mechanics, poets, and aunties in Ma'an rebuilt an intercity bus so it can host math, radio, and textile pods on wheels.

For years, students in Jordan's desert towns traveled hours to reach Noor programming. Now the programming rolls directly to their doorstep.

Published

Featuring

This update showcases how Noor's English programmes scale—linking community infrastructure, donor support, and educator leadership to rich language learning experiences.

The Study Bus began as a joke: "What if the classroom came to us?" Within six months a team of teenage mechanics, their uncles, and the neighbourhood sewing collective turned a retired bus into a fully outfitted mobile lab. Noor supplied the curriculum kits, solar consultants, and safety certification; the community led the build.

Inside, the floor is divided into three pods. Fold-out tables and whiteboards anchor the math studio. The middle pod stores recording equipment and acoustic panels for radio diaries. The rear pod hosts sewing machines, a dye station, and lockable cabinets for textile arts. Every surface includes QR codes that link to quick-start tutorials in Arabic and English.

The bus parks at a new location every two days following a route agreed on by a youth steering committee. A small stipend fund keeps diesel costs covered until the solar retrofit finishes next month. Since launch, 214 students have used the labs, and 87% report they would have skipped programming if transportation wasn't provided.
