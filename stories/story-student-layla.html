Layla Codes Irrigation Sensors Between Care Shifts

The 17-year-old rotates between babysitting her siblings and maintaining solar-powered soil probes that keep the community garden alive.

Layla joined Noor after her family's small farm outside Kenitra lost most of its harvest to heat waves. She now prototypes water-saving tech with aunties and cousins.

Category
Student Voices
Published
May 3, 2024
Location
Kenitra, Morocco
Featuring
Layla Idrissi (Climate Apprentice)

Every activity in this Student Voice feature is anchored to Noor's English language pathway, so learners log new vocabulary, practice academic sentence frames, and rehearse oral fluency while they build.

Turning chores into code

Every dawn Layla walks the irrigation channels with her grandmother, measuring how much water leaks from clay pipes. During Noor's afternoon climate pod she translates those notes into sensor code. Using recycled phone chargers, she and her cohort soldered solar-powered soil probes that text moisture alerts to a shared WhatsApp list.

The alerts include a "care rotation" so each cousin knows which patch needs shade cloth, mulching, or storytelling for the kids who play nearby. "I wanted the tech to feel as tender as our family calendar," Layla says.

What the crew built

  • 12 open-source probes that can be buried inside planters or irrigation ditches.
  • A bilingual dashboard that visualizes soil data alongside oral histories about the plots.
  • A rotating "sisters in STEM" workshop where Layla teaches sensor basics to younger girls.

Community impact

Neighbors report a 30% reduction in wasted water, and the once-dusty alley beside the garden now hosts weekly maintenance circles. Layla's grandmother keeps a printed log of every alert, taping them next to family photos. "It proves that science is just another form of storytelling," she says.
