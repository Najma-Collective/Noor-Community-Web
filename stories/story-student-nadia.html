Nadia's Mural Studio Doubles as Language Lab

A trilingual artist in Marseille transforms shuttered storefronts into vocabulary walls for recently arrived classmates.

Nadia spends her mornings in lycée and her afternoons mixing pigments with aunties who once painted protest banners in Algiers. Together they turned an alley of metal shutters into Noor's newest language lab.

Category
Student Voices
Published
May 12, 2024
Location
Marseille, France
Featuring
Nadia Cheriet (Youth Mentor)

Every activity in this Student Voice feature is anchored to Noor's English language pathway, so learners log new vocabulary, practice academic sentence frames, and rehearse oral fluency while they build.

Nadia arrived in Marseille with more sketchbooks than suitcases. French was her third language, and school felt like a hallway of locked doors until a Noor facilitator invited her to help translate murals for a community night market. The quick commission snowballed into a weekly workshop where Nadia now guides refugee classmates and neighbourhood aunties through collaborative mural cycles.

Every mural is structured like a mini language curriculum. Nadia sketches silhouettes first, then layers bilingual vocabulary, phonetic spellings, and QR codes that link to short pronunciation clips recorded by students. By the time the shutter is filled, youth have collectively practiced reading aloud, tracing letters, and composing their own phrases in Arabic, French, and Comorian.

The storefronts are more than canvases, they are rehearsal spaces. Nadia uses painter's tape grids to host spelling bees, sets up portable speakers for poetry, and ends each session with a "brush circle" where participants name something they learned. Local shop owners now leave stools and water outside because the murals pull families into the alley every weekend.

What Nadia's crew built

  • A rotation of six shutters that refresh every eight weeks with new vocabulary themes.
  • An open-source stencil library featuring community-drawn alphabets.
  • A WhatsApp hotline where voice notes become pronunciation guides.

"We are writing our presence into the city," Nadia says. "The shutters close each night, but the words stay in our mouths." Her next goal is to host a twilight mural studio that pairs older migrants with primary school students so that every design doubles as an oral history interview.
