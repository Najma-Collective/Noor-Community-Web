Launching the Noor Learning Lab Network

Five partner schools are piloting trauma-informed math and literacy studios, sharing data through our new learning network.

Educators across Brazil, Jordan, Kenya, and the UK have joined Noor to co-design living laboratories. The goal: adapt our healing-centred pedagogy to their contexts while contributing new insights back to the network.

Category

Published

Participating sites

This update showcases how Noor's English programmes scale: linking community infrastructure, donor support, and educator leadership to rich language learning experiences.

What is the Learning Lab Network?

The network is a six-month collaboration with schools that serve displaced or first-generation students. Each site receives coaching, curriculum kits, and stipends to compensate educators who share their data. In return, they agree to host at least two public learning exchanges.

Focus areas

  • Math studios. Iterations on our “Liberatory Algebra” sequence with a focus on tactile materials.
  • Literacy studios. Community journalism projects that blend academic writing with civic storytelling.
  • Wellbeing routines. Breathwork and somatic practices that can be facilitated by students themselves.

Shared infrastructure

We set up a secure portal where educators upload observation notes, attendance snapshots, and short audio reflections. The portal tags content by theme so that patterns (like increased attendance after a particular ritual) surface quickly.

Timeline

Labs began in April and will conclude with a digital showcase in September. In June we will host an open webinar so other schools can learn from the first round of prototypes.

“We are not prescribing a single model,” explains programme lead Nuria Castro. “We are convening a circle of practitioners who are willing to share the messy middle.”

How to get involved

Interested schools can express interest via our partner page. Philanthropic partners can underwrite a lab by emailing partnerships@noor-community.org.
