Mapping Futures with the KinetiQ Youth Fellows

Thirteen secondary students from Nairobi co-designed civic capstones rooted in public health, climate storytelling, and mutual aid.

The KinetiQ Youth Fellowship partnered with Noor to prototype a student-led research sprint. Over five days, fellows mapped their city, interviewed neighbours, and built prototypes that reflect the community issues they care about most.

Category
Student Voices
Published
March 21, 2024
Location
Nairobi, Kenya
Partners
KinetiQ Labs + Noor Community

This update showcases how Noor's English programmes scale: linking community infrastructure, donor support, and educator leadership to rich language learning experiences.

Day 1: Sense-making and values

We began with a city walk through Eastleigh, documenting the informal health networks that families rely on. Fellows carried audio recorders and sketchbooks, capturing how kiosks double as information hubs. The walk ended with a gallery of assets: everything from aunties offering first aid tips to boda boda riders who deliver medicine.

Day 2–3: Civic investigation labs

Fellows split into three labs: climate storytelling, mutual aid logistics, and public health communication. Each lab was guided by a Noor facilitator and a community researcher. Students practiced interview techniques, data visualization, and rapid prototyping.

  • Climate storytellers wrote bilingual micro zines distributed at local markets.
  • Mutual aid teams mapped cold storage options for insulin delivery.
  • Health communicators co-created WhatsApp voice notes to debunk rumours.

Day 4: Prototyping with dignity

Instead of pitching to a panel, fellows hosted a community review. Families circulated between project stations and offered “warm, cool, bright” feedback. This format honoured interdependence and allowed quieter students to lead deep conversations at their tables.

“We wanted adults to respond as collaborators, not judges,” said Maryam, one of the fellows. “That changed the questions we asked of ourselves.”

Day 5: Commitments and care

The sprint closed with a commitments circle. Each fellow named one relationship they plan to nurture to keep their project alive: a teacher, a sibling, a market vendor. Noor facilitators will continue coaching them virtually over the next quarter while they apply for micro-grants.

The KinetiQ team has already requested a second sprint in July, this time inviting alumni to mentor the next cohort. Our curriculum team is preparing an open-source facilitation kit so other hubs can host their own civic capstones.
