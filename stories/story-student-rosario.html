Rosario Prints Justice Posters on Her Rooftop

An elevator-less apartment becomes a civics studio where elders, teens, and toddlers pull screen prints side by side.

Rosario's abuela was a union printer. When the family lost access to a community center, they moved the press to their rooftop and invited the block.

Category
Student Voices
Published
April 24, 2024
Location
Buenos Aires, Argentina
Featuring
Rosario Álvarez (Print Lead)

Every activity in this Student Voice feature is anchored to Noor's English language pathway, so learners log new vocabulary, practice academic sentence frames, and rehearse oral fluency while they build.

Building an accessible studio

Neighbors contributed plywood, tarps, and fairy lights to shield the press from wind. Rosario mapped out stations using sidewalk chalk so wheelchair users could see paths clearly. A pulley system now lifts ink buckets, saving everyone's backs.

Posters as curriculum

Each poster includes a QR code linking to Noor's civic glossary. Youth interview tenants, then translate demands into bold typography. Printing becomes a math lesson when they calculate ink ratios and margin widths. Rosario says, "The press is our group chat."

Rooftop diplomacy

After every print run, participants sit in a circle to share where the posters will travel: markets, bus stops, classrooms. Those debriefs surface safety concerns and ideas for mutual aid. The rooftop now hosts Saturday teach-ins led by aunties who once printed strike leaflets.
