Iman's Zine Club Doubles as a Study Hall

A crew of girls in Toronto writes accountability plans, comics, and exam prep tips inside handbound booklets.

Iman wanted a space where friends could process harm and pass classes without adult surveillance. She borrowed Noor's button maker and got to work.

Category
Student Voices
Published
April 18, 2024
Location
Toronto, Canada
Featuring
Iman Farah (Restorative Lead)

Every activity in this Student Voice feature is anchored to Noor's English language pathway, so learners log new vocabulary, practice academic sentence frames, and rehearse oral fluency while they build.

Restorative rituals on paper

Each zine begins with a circle check-in recorded in the margins. Members script "repair recipes" that include action steps, playlists, and elder advice. The pages are photocopied so everyone takes home the plan.

Study skills woven in

Halfway through each session, the crew transitions to study hall. They glue in exam calendars, annotate practice problems, and mark moments when they need care breaks. The zine becomes both planner and emotional ledger.

Community response

Teachers now request bundles of zines to spark conversations about accountability. Iman is building a "repair library" so younger students can remix the templates. "We aren’t just venting," she says. "We’re publishing the way we want to be treated."
