Student Stories

Voices of Narrative Control.

Each story is a case study of how students exercise agency, cultivate professional skills, and protect culture through learning.

Fatima's Story: From Classroom Project to Real-World Grant

"Before taking the Business English course, I felt like my career dreams were always one step away. I understood the concepts of grant writing and strategic planning, but the language barrier (especially the professional tone and specific terminology) was a huge hurdle.

Everything changed with the 'NGO in Nablus' project. The task was incredibly realistic: we had to act as a youth-led NGO, analyse two potential grant templates, and prepare a 2-minute justification for our choice. We practised English whilst learning to think like a professional in the NGO sector. We learned how to use the Second Conditional to explain the hypothetical advantages of choosing grants like the 'Al-Quds Innovation Fund' or the 'Digital Futures Foundation'.

Working through the main task of choosing and justifying a grant proposal gave me the confidence I never had. We practised using precise terminology like 'funding stream' and 'measurable outcome'. The process of debating, analysing pros and cons, and presenting a formal justification completely transformed my approach.

I'm now applying for a real international development grant for a local education initiative. The initial complexity feels manageable because the Business English course taught me how to strategise and articulate a winning proposal. I feel ready to secure funding and make a real difference in my community."

Noor's Story: Education as an Act of Preservation

“My roots run through Gaza, from my grandmother's stories of displacement to my father's belief that daughters should be educated. Growing up, my passion for education became a bridge to the outside world. I studied English at Al-Azhar University in Gaza, graduating with a Bachelor's in English Language and Teaching Methods, and found fulfillment in teaching.

When our peaceful life was shattered by conflict, we lost our home and sought refuge, struggling for basic necessities. Despite these challenges, we held on to our dreams and our culture, finding strength in our heritage and community.

Joining the Noor Community was a vital step in reclaiming my professional identity. It was more than a class; it was a network of solidarity. The 'healing-centred' approach gave me a space to be an educator and a learner again, not just a survivor.

Today, I teach Arabic and English, sharing my story and culture with students worldwide. My experience has reinforced my belief in education as an act of preservation and resilience. I hope to one day open a cultural learning center for displaced families. Noor Community helped me keep that dream alive, reminding me that even in exile, we carry our identity and our future with us.”
